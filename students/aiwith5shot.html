What you need

  • An AI chatbot you have access to, such as ChatJimmy, MagicSchool AI, or Gemini.
  • Your 5-shot data from your time with Data Dunkers. Your leader will share the spreadsheet. No data yet? Use the example dataset below.

Get your data (do this once)

  1. Open your 5-shot spreadsheet.
  2. Click File, then Download, then Comma Separated Values (.csv).
  3. Open the downloaded file. Select all of it and copy.

Each row is one turn: a player's initials, the shot distance in feet, and how many shots they made out of 5. One player has several rows, one for each spot. Some players did not get to every spot. That is fine, and it is worth watching how the AI handles it.

Three rules for your chatbot

  1. Start a new chat for every challenge. Long chats get worse.
  2. Paste the prompt first, then your data below it, then send.
  3. Check its claims against the spreadsheet. Confident does not mean correct.

Challenge 1 · Do this first

Meet the Shot Data

Goal: Find out what the AI understands about your data, and catch what it gets wrong.

  1. Start a new chat.
  2. Copy the prompt below and paste it into your chatbot.
  3. Paste your data directly below the prompt. Send.
  4. Read the answer together. Did it understand that each player has several rows?
Prompt · copy this 
I am pasting shooting data from students at a basketball data event. Each player took 5 shots from each of 5 spots: 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10 feet. Each row is one turn: the player's initials, the shot distance in feet, and how many shots they made out of 5. Some players did not get to every spot. Explain what you understand about this dataset in simple words. Then tell me one thing that looks interesting and one thing that looks odd or messy. Here is the data:
Check it: This data has quirks: repeated rows, missing spots, messy initials. Did the AI notice any of them? Did you?
Challenge 2

Shooting Profiles

Goal: Find the snipers and the close-range specialists.

  1. New chat. Paste the prompt, then your data. Send.
  2. Pick two players it names and find their rows in the spreadsheet. Is the AI right?
  3. Ask a follow up: which player surprised you the most, and why?
Prompt · copy this 
Here is shooting data from students at a basketball data event. Each row is one player's turn: initials, shot distance in feet, and shots made out of 5. Find the players who stay accurate as the distance grows, and the players who are strong up close but fade from far away. Name the players and point to the rows that show it. Do not invent numbers. Here is the data:
Check it: A profile built on one great turn is thin evidence. How many rows back up each claim?
Challenge 3

The Training Plan

Goal: Get a training plan built on your real numbers, then find its blind spots.

  1. Pick a player. Yourself is the best choice if this is your own data.
  2. New chat. Fill the initials into the prompt, paste your data, send.
  3. Read the plan. For every recommendation, find the numbers behind it.
  4. Ask your group: would a real coach agree with this plan?
Prompt · fill in the initials, then copy 
Here is shooting data from students at a basketball data event. Each row is one player's turn: initials, shot distance in feet, and shots made out of 5. I am player [INITIALS]. Look at my results at each distance. What should I train on first, and why? Point to my real numbers for every recommendation. Then tell me the important things this data cannot know about my shooting. Here is the data:
Check it: What did the AI say the data cannot know? Form, fatigue, pressure, and effort are not columns. Neither is having a bad day.
Challenge 4

Game Day

Goal: Draft two balanced teams, then run an imaginary shootout between them.

  1. New chat. Paste the draft prompt, then your data. Send.
  2. Review the rosters. You are the general managers: overrule any pick you disagree with, and tell the AI why.
  3. When both teams look fair, send the game prompt in the same chat.
  4. Enjoy the play by play. Then do the check below.
Draft prompt · copy this 
Here is shooting data from students at a basketball data event. Each row is one player's turn: initials, shot distance in feet, and shots made out of 5. Split the players into two balanced teams. Do not calculate scores. Look at who is strong up close and who is strong from deep, and give each team a mix of both. Explain each pick in one short line. Give each team a fun, school friendly name. Here is the data:
Game prompt · send in the same chat 
Now run an imaginary shootout game between the two teams. Each player shoots once from all five spots. Base what happens on their real data: a player who went 5 for 5 from 2 feet should almost never miss there, and a player who went 0 for 5 from 10 feet should not suddenly get hot. Give us short, fun play by play commentary and a final score.
Check it: Pick two moments from the commentary and find the numbers behind them. Did anyone perform way outside their data? Run the game again. Different result? Talk about why.
Challenge 5

Sign a Pro

Goal: Sign one NBA or WNBA player to your team, and defend the pick with data.

  1. New chat. Paste the scouting prompt, then your data. It will tell you what kind of shooter your team is missing.
  2. As a group, pick your pro. Any NBA or WNBA player you want.
  3. Fill your player's name into the prediction prompt and send it in the same chat.
  4. Debate the AI's predictions. Would your pro really never miss? Compare with our guesses in the wildcard table below.
  5. Bonus: add your pro's wildcard rows to your data and rerun Game Day with them in the lineup.
Scouting prompt · copy this 
Here is my team's shooting data from a basketball data event. Each row is one player's turn: initials, shot distance in feet, and shots made out of 5. Our team can sign one professional basketball player, NBA or WNBA. Based on what our data says we are missing, what kind of shooter do we need most? Describe the type of player. Do not name anyone yet. Here is the data:
Prediction prompt · fill in the name, send in the same chat 
We are signing [PLAYER NAME]. Predict what they would score in our challenge: 5 shots from each spot at 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10 feet. Give a number out of 5 for each spot and explain your reasoning. Be honest: would they really never miss?

Challenge 6

The Trade

Goal: Make the AI argue both sides of a trade, then rule on it yourselves.

  1. Two groups (or two halves of your group) each nominate one player to trade.
  2. New chat. Fill both names into the prompt, paste your data, send.
  3. Read both arguments out loud, courtroom style.
  4. Vote: does the trade happen? Majority rules.
Prompt · fill in the players, then copy 
Here is shooting data from students at a basketball data event. Each row is one player's turn: initials, shot distance in feet, and shots made out of 5. Two teams are discussing a trade: [PLAYER 1] for [PLAYER 2]. First argue the case FOR the trade, then argue the case AGAINST it, using only the numbers in the data. Do not pick a winner. End with one question each team should ask before deciding. Here is the data:
Check it: Did both arguments use real numbers? Which side was stronger, and who says so: you, or the AI?
Challenge 7

The Rematch

Goal: Predict tomorrow's scores, then question how much 5 shots can really tell you.

  1. New chat. Paste the prompt, then your data. Send.
  2. Pick one prediction you disagree with and argue back with data.
  3. If you get the chance, actually reshoot and compare. Reality is the best fact checker.
Prompt · copy this 
Here is shooting data from students at a basketball data event. Each row is one player's turn: initials, shot distance in feet, and shots made out of 5. Imagine everyone shoots again tomorrow: 5 shots from each of the 5 spots. Predict what would change and what would stay the same, and explain your reasoning. Then answer honestly: how much can 5 shots per spot really tell us about a player? Here is the data:
Check it: If a player went 3 for 5, are they really a 60 percent shooter? What would make you more confident: more shots, more days, or both?
Challenge 8

The Broadcast

Goal: Get a highlight recap of shot day, then fact-check every claim in it.

  1. New chat. Paste the prompt, then your data. Send.
  2. Read the broadcast out loud in your best commentator voice.
  3. Fact-check it as a group: every claim must trace to a real number.
  4. Flag anything invented and make the AI correct it.
Prompt · copy this 
Here is shooting data from students at a basketball data event. Each row is one player's turn: initials, shot distance in feet, and shots made out of 5. You are a fun, energetic sports commentator. Write a short highlight recap of shot day. Every claim must trace back to a real number in the data. Keep it kind: celebrate great shooting, never mock a miss. Here is the data:
Check it: Commentators love a good story. Which lines were backed by data, and which were just good television?
